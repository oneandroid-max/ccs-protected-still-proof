The self-defending proof, the page

The photo below is stored only encrypted. The key comes from this link and the PIN you type, and nothing else. The photo is drawn on the screen, never served as a file, in two alternating frames that your eye blends into the picture while a screenshot catches only one noisy frame. Every copy that leaves this page, by screenshot, recording or share, carries an invisible mark naming the viewer and the hour. No vendor service is involved on any platform.

Try it

  1. Type the PIN and wait until "Playing" reads yes. You should see the photo with a fine shimmer.
  2. Take a screenshot with the device's buttons and open it from your gallery. The photo area should be a noisy, broken copy; the rest of the page looks normal.
  3. Start a screen recording, stop it, and play it back. It shows the frames flickering; a recording can be averaged back into the photo, and that copy still carries your mark.
  4. Tap "Stop the alternation". The photo becomes a still. A screenshot now captures it cleanly, and that copy still carries your mark.
  5. Download the share card: the honest copy a family is given, credited and marked.

What this cannot do

This page cannot make a capture come out black: the browser hands every frame to the device, and nothing inside a page can refuse the device's own capture. The Android viewer, an installed app of ours, does refuse it. A screen recording of this page captures both frames and averaging them recovers the photo. A camera can photograph the screen. A developer console can read the canvas. A device that limits animation, such as an iPhone in Low Power Mode, is shown the marked still instead, and a screenshot captures it cleanly. Every one of those copies still carries the viewer's mark. The promise of this process is that a leak is traceable and a casual capture is worthless, not that a capture is impossible.

The share the family is given

The share card is a smaller copy with the studio's name and a place for the gallery link, carrying the same invisible mark. It is the easy path: free, credited, and traceable, so there is no reason to capture the screen.
