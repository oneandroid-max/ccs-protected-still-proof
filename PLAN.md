# The plan — the self-defending proof

This file is the living plan for the proof. It is kept current with every push, so anyone can read
where the work stands without asking. Dates are UTC.

## The goal, in the owner's words (2026-10-10)

> "We are setting out to create and not copy nor use third party interventions, a process that will
> block users from stealing images, on all viable formats."

> "The image needs to appear undistorted but an attempt at copy of it via print screen or long press
> and save will give nothing in return."

The two places that do not yet give that result, a black capture on the web and a guarantee on
iPhone, are part of the target: *"we are going to get here, we're not there yet, but we are."*

## What is ruled out

- Every vendor protection path: FairPlay, Widevine, PlayReady and any DRM licence service. The first
  proof used Widevine and is kept at `widevine/` only as a comparison.
- A distorted or noisy picture for the viewer. The viewer sees the photo clean.
- A copy that is merely traceable. The second proof (the page at the root) makes a capture noisy and
  marked; it does not block, and blocking is the goal.

## What is ours in every format

The encrypted file (`photo.enc`, AES-256-GCM), the key that exists in no file (link secret plus PIN
through PBKDF2-SHA256), the viewer, the invisible mark naming the viewer and the hour, the readout
that prints only what the device measured, and the fail-closed rule: where the device cannot refuse a
capture, the full photo is never drawn.

The refusal itself can only come from the part of a device that draws the screen. Our viewers ask the
device for it; no outside service is in the chain.

- 2026-10-11T01:01Z: **decoy frame, measured in the sandbox before it reached the owner: no.** Built (`web/research/decoy/`): frame A = photo + pattern, frame B = photo − pattern, a structured multi-scale pattern balanced within blocks so no area flashes. Headless Chromium, two settings (amplitude 110 at 32-px blocks, 127 at 64-px): the pattern's strength after the range cap came to 25–27 of 255, a single frame sits 20 dB from the photo, and a 12-px blur of the frame sits 26–33 dB from the blurred photo; the face is plainly visible in the single frame. The reason is a bound, not a setting: both frames must stay inside 0–255 and average to the photo, so the disruption in any one frame can never exceed the photo's own distance from black and white, |D| ≤ min(P, 255−P). Hiding a face in a single frame would need the pattern several times the photo's contrast, which the bound allows only if the photo shown to the viewer is squashed to a fraction of its contrast; the viewer's picture and the thief's confusion trade one for one. With this, the two things the eye gets that a capture does not, time and brightness, are both measured closed on the web: the time route is bounded by the range of a pixel, the brightness route is squeezed into the capture by the phone.

## Testing the Android viewer

1. On the phone, open https://oneandroid-max.github.io/ccs-protected-still-proof/dist/ccs-proof-viewer.apk and install it (allow installs from the browser when asked; it installs over the previous version).
2. Open "CCS Proof Viewer", paste the link key, type the PIN, tap "Show the photo". The photo appears clean.
3. Take a screenshot. Start and stop a screen recording. Cast the screen if a TV is near. Open the recent-apps view. Long-press the photo.
4. Read the readout: "Capture refusal: ON" is the flag read back from the window; every other line is measured on the phone.

## Testing on the Android test platform (version 0.2 and later)

1. In the viewer, tap "Open the Android test platform". The link key and PIN carry over.
2. Variables: the "Capture refusal" switch (on or off, live); the content buttons: "Native photo", "trace/", "decoy/", "above white", "bench", "door", or any address in the box.
3. For each combination: show the content, take a screenshot, read the readout, send the screenshot. A black or refused capture with the refusal on and a normal capture with it off is the phone measuring the flag; the content under it is the variable.
