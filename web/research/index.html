Can a web page make a capture come out black without a vendor path?

Candidates 1 to 9 are tried one at a time on a stock browser with no vendor DRM, and the result is what a capture holds, or that the device refused one (candidate 7); a pass is a black capture, and none of them is expected to pass: the point is to close the question with evidence, not opinion. Candidate 10, the browser's own vendor DRM path, is on the table for comparison; it is the one measured black. Results are logged in PLAN.md with the device and the browser where each was reported (the colleague's copy in row 10 came with neither); the owner's own screenshots are recorded there in his words and in what was read from them, with no hash; a capture's hash is logged only for captures taken on GitHub's runners.

Why none of candidates 1 to 9 is expected to pass

The browser hands every frame it draws to the device's compositor, and the device's screenshot reads that compositor. Only a surface the device marks as protected is left out, and on the web a page gets such a surface only through the vendor DRM paths: candidate 10, route 1, the one measured black (a private tab's secure flag, candidate 7, is the browser's and the viewer's, not the page's). A page can change what it draws (candidate 6 does), where it draws (2, 3, 5) and how the bytes arrive (4); it cannot change what the device does with the frame once drawn.
